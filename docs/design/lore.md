# Experiment Undead: Lore Bible

Source: copy of the design doc as of Nov 14, 2026. Where this file and the code differ, the code and `docs/DECISIONS.md` win.

## The premise

A cold-loving bog organism called **Fenlight** was kept asleep in freezing labs under the Ardennes. One winter night the power failed, the labs warmed up, and it woke. Now the dead walk with green light in their veins, a military cordon has sealed the region, and a handful of survivors in a ridge-top village called **Lantern Post** send salvagers back into the zone for gear, data and answers.

The one-line pitch: *the cold kept it asleep, and someone turned off the cold.* That is why the art direction is Cold Containment: cold means safe, warmth means danger, green means Fenlight.

## What caused the outbreak

**Fenlight** is a glowing green mould from the Grauwald Fen. It colonises nerves, rebuilds damaged tissue, and keeps a body moving after death so it can spread by bite. It goes dormant below about -10 °C, which is why every lab that held it was kept freezing.

The outbreak was not an accident. The power cut at Kessel Dam was manual, and the person who threw the switch is the game's central mystery (see Dr. Maren Aust under Factions).

**Why waves.** Infected hosts pulse in sync, like fireflies. Each pulse pulls every host within kilometres toward warm bodies. Players call one pulse a **round**, and the pulses get stronger over a night. The short quiet between rounds is the lull between pulses.

| When | What happened | Where players see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1951 | A doctor at Grauwald Sanatorium treats tuberculosis patients with a fen mould extract. They recover, then stop staying dead. | Grauwald Sanatorium |
| 1954 | The worst cases are walled up in Ward Zero and the sanatorium closes. | Ward Zero secret |
| 1962 | A Cold War bunker is dug into the pine ridge, later abandoned. | Halcyon Labs layout |
| 7 years before | Oriel Biotechnic buys the bunker and the sanatorium records, and starts Project HALCYON. | Halcyon Labs notes |
| Day 0 | Kessel Dam's turbines are shut down on the coldest night of the year. The labs warm up and Fenlight wakes. | Kessel Dam, Halcyon Labs |
| Day 3 | Evacuation trains at Marrow Junction are overrun. | Marrow Junction |
| Day 9 | The Cordon seals the region. | Radio, town |
| Day 200 (now) | Deep winter. Lantern Post holds on and sends salvagers out. | Lantern Post |

The year is never named. Tech is modern military, so the present just feels like now.

## Factions

Five groups, but players only ever fight the infected. The rest exist in notes, radio and town.

| Faction | Who they are | What they want | How players meet them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lit** (the infected) | Fenlight hosts. Eyes and veins glow green. Slower in cold, faster near heat. | To spread. | Every round. |
| **Oriel Biotechnic** | Private biotech firm that ran Project HALCYON. Gone now, but its machines still run. | Healing soldiers that never stay down. | Memos, staff logs, the machines, the Prototype rarity. |
| **The Cordon** | Military force guarding the zone's edge. Shoots anyone who walks out. | Keep it in. Buy Oriel data quietly. | Radio between rounds, supply drops, the liaison in town. |
| **Lantern Post** | Survivors on the cold ridge. The players are its salvagers. | Survive the winter and find a cure. | The town hub and its NPCs. |
| **The Wick** | A small group led by Dr. Maren Aust, ex-Oriel lead scientist. They think Fenlight is the next step for humans, not a disease. | To let it spread. | Never seen. Graffiti, stolen radio frequencies, Aust's recordings. |

**Dr. Maren Aust** is the thread that runs through all four maps. Her notes suggest she cut the power on Day 0, but the last ones hint she was trying to stop something worse. Each map's secret gives one more piece. Leaving her fate open keeps room for later maps. The long campaign answers it in Act III.

## Why the machines exist

Every machine is leftover Oriel equipment that still works because Fenlight has grown into it. That is why they glow green and why they only wake up when the power is on.

| Machine | In-world reason | Lore detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Power switch** | Restarts the bunker's backup generators. | Power brings back the cold and the machines, but also the lights the Lit are drawn to. |
| **Serums** (Med Dispensers) | Oriel's field doses: tamed, weak Fenlight that boosts the body for a short time. Ironhide, Quickhands, Overclock and the rest were trial product names. | The body rejects them when you go down, which is why you lose all Serums. Second Wind was the drug HALCYON was really after. |
| **Specimen Vault** | Oriel's automated sample and seized-gear archive. It hands out whatever is on the shelf it reaches. | It moves between spots because Fenlight shifts the shelves. The Vault "breathing" before it moves is the mould. |
| **Reforger** | Oriel's press for coating metal in Fenlight-cured alloy. | Reforged guns hit harder because the alloy is alive. Higher tiers glow more green. |
| **Prototype items** | Oriel test weapons still carrying live Fenlight. | Explains the bio-green rarity colour #8DFF3A. |
| **Exfil** | Lantern Post pickup crews (boat, truck, helicopter, hatch). | Each map's exfil is a crew that took real risk to get there. |

**Why you keep loot when you die.** Every salvager wears a Lantern Post tether tag. When you go down for good, a pickup crew drags you and your pack home. You wake in the town infirmary. Nothing needs explaining in-game beyond one line from the doctor.

## The four maps

Players go forward in map order but backward in time: Halcyon shows the result, Grauwald shows the origin. Each secret adds one piece of the Maren Aust story.

### 1. Halcyon Labs: where it broke out

The HALCYON bunker, where Oriel grew Fenlight in cold tanks. On Day 0 the cold failed and the staff turned first. The last supply truck burning at the Loading Dock was the final run out.

**Secret: Signal in the Static.** The three radio tones are Aust's private call sign. The Cold Room is her hidden lab. The Cryo Lance on the rack is the tool she built to freeze hosts, which proves she was fighting Fenlight, not just freeing it. The Halcyon Pin is an Oriel staff pin.

### 2. Kessel Dam: who cut the power

The dam powered the region and Oriel's private cold line. The turbine hall shows a manual shutdown, not a failure. The Spillway flood event is the dam still fighting its own broken controls.

**Secret: Drowned Samples.** Weeks before Day 0, Oriel sank sample jars in the reservoir to hide them from an inspection. Shooting the three jars drains the cell that held the rest. The cache holds Oriel gear, and a log there shows the shutdown used Aust's key card, but on a shift she was not on. The Kessel Gauge is the turbine pressure gauge, stuck at zero.

### 3. Marrow Junction: how it spread

The rail yard where evacuation trains gathered on Day 3. An infected passenger got on, and the trains never left. The shunting loco event uses the one engine that still runs. This is the most human map: suitcases, kids' drawings, notes taped to lamp posts.

**Secret: Lost Property.** The four luggage tags belong to evacuees, each with a short note on the back. Together they show an Oriel courier smuggled a Siphon prototype onto the train in someone else's bag. The back room is where it ended up. The Junction Lamp is the yard's old signal lamp.

### 4. Grauwald Sanatorium: where it started

A 1920s tuberculosis sanatorium on the fen edge, built for cold-air cures. In 1951 its doctor treated patients with fen mould. They got better, then came back after death. The Lockdown event uses the original 1950s fire doors, built to keep patients in.

**Secret: Ward Zero.** The five green numbers make up the patient number of the first host, still sealed on the lowest floor. Aust's last recording is found here: she came back to the source to find out why the first host never rotted. The Grauwald Lantern is a night nurse's lantern, and it is where Lantern Post got its name, since the town's founders were nurses who fled here in 1954.

## The long campaign

The story is built to run for many maps, like the acts of a classic loot game. Players gather in Lantern Post, squad up, and deploy into maps full of the Lit and bosses. Each act answers one big question and ends with a boss. Adding story means adding an act or a chapter, never rewriting the ones before.

Decided by the person (2026-11-17): acts of 4 maps with a boss on every map, bosses reached by a mid-run descent, and bosses that are named Lit with a human story. Act II and III map and boss names are outlines and can change.

### How it is shaped

- **Act** = one region of the zone, 4 maps, one big question. The 4th map's boss is the **act boss** and the story payoff.
- **Chapter** = one map. It keeps the full wave loop, its secret and its signature event. It also gets one **map boss**.
- **Act I goes back in time** (the four maps we have). **Later acts go outward in space**, ring by ring, from the valley to the Cordon wall and then into the deep fen.
- Every map stays fully playable as a normal wave map. The boss and the story are extras on top, never a gate to having fun.

### How a boss fight happens

The pattern is the Halcyon Labs Sublevel that is already planned.

1. From round 20, with power on, a **descent** point opens on the map (a freight elevator, a turbine shaft, a service pit). It costs points to open.
2. The squad rides down to the map's **Sublevel**, a small arena below the map. The waves stop for the descent.
3. The boss fight has 2 phases (act bosses: 3). Each phase has one clear tell and one counter, like the Brute.
4. Win: a guaranteed boss drop, a lore entry, and an exfil ride straight from the Sublevel. Or ride back up and keep surviving for a bigger score.
5. Lose: the run ends as a normal death. You keep your loot (tether tag rule).

Only the squad that rides down fights. In co-op, players who stay up keep fighting waves until the boss is dead.

### Boss rules

- **Every boss was a person.** Someone from the notes, the secrets or the town's stories, grown huge by Fenlight. Finding their notes before the fight tells you their weak spot.
- **Only the infected.** Players never fight humans. The Wick and the Cordon stay in notes, radio and town.
- **Cold is the weapon.** Every boss gets slower or exposed in the cold (vents, frozen water, cryo tanks) and faster near heat. This keeps the core rule: cold means safe.
- **Readable.** One sound and one shape per attack, telegraphed for at least 1 second. No instant kills.
- **One chase item each.** Each boss has one Legendary that only it drops (ties into F-31 Legendaries and traits).

### Act IV and on

After the fen, the pulse is heard beyond the Cordon: other Oriel sites, other cold places that held samples. Each new act is a new region with its own question. Nothing in Acts I to III should close that door.

### How the hub grows with the story

Lantern Post is one town for the whole game. It grows as the story moves, so returning players see their progress.

- **Each player sees their own town.** The town's look and its unlocked services follow your own character's story progress, like a classic loot game. Nothing about it is shared server state, so 8 players at different acts can stand in the same town.
- **One new lamp line per act.** Clearing an act lights a new row of sodium lamps up the ridge and opens one new part of town.
- **One new person per act.** Act I: Dr. Corvelyn's doubts about Aust pay off. Act II: a Cordon deserter arrives and runs the endgame board. Act III: a former Wick believer arrives and explains the fen.
- **Barks and the Wall of Finds** grow with every chapter: 3 to 5 lines per NPC per milestone, one card per secret and per boss.
- **Pell's deploy board** shows acts as columns and chapters as rows, so a new map is one more row.

### Endgame and repeatable play

Once Act I is beaten, there is always another reason for one more run. None of these need a new kind of system: they reuse waves, bosses, contracts and loot.

| Hook | What it is | Lore reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Endless waves** | Every map stays an endless wave map with best-round records. | The pulses never stop. |
| **Heat levels** | Difficulty steps (Heat 1 to 10) unlocked by beating an act. More health and speed, higher item level and rarity floor. | Warmer means stronger Fenlight. |
| **Boss hunts** | Contracts to kill a beaten boss again, starting the run at round 15 with power on. Each boss keeps its own chase Legendary. | The Lit keep rebuilding the host. |
| **Echoes** | A weekly version of one map with 2 or 3 rule changes, with its own Hall of Records board. | Pell picks up a strange pulse pattern. |
| **Contracts** | The daily and weekly board from the town design. | Lieutenant Raes, later the Cordon deserter. |
| **Codex and Wall of Finds** | Find every note, secret and boss card per act. | The full story is the reward. |

### How a new chapter or act slots in

A new map is a chapter. Adding one is a checklist, not a rewrite:

1. Which act and which question it helps answer.
2. A setting in the act's region, and what the Lit wear there.
3. One signature event and one secret (one piece of the act's story).
4. One map boss: who they were, their 2 phases, their cold weakness, their chase item.
5. About 12 notes and 8 radio lines as lore files.
6. Town barks for the chapter's milestones, a Wall of Finds card for the secret and the boss.
7. A row on Pell's deploy board.

A new act is 4 chapters plus: an act boss with 3 phases, one new lamp line and one new person in town, and one more Heat step.

**Unlock rule.** Act I keeps the decided rule (round 15 on the previous map, or level 8, 18, 28). From Act II on, an act opens when you beat the previous act boss; inside an act, the next map opens at round 15 on the previous one. Squads can still play any map the host has unlocked. Provisional.

**For the build.** This section is story and design only. The code for acts, descents and bosses waits for the roadmap row "Campaign: acts, descents and bosses" and its plan. Keep the data simple when that plan is written: an act and chapter number on the map's data, and bosses as enemy data with phases, not a new framework.

## Lantern Post and its people

Lantern Post is an old border-post village on the highest, coldest ridge in the zone. It survives because the Lit slow down in the cold, and because the villagers keep sodium lamps burning on every roof so salvagers can find their way home at night. Orange sodium light means safe; that is the town's look against the green of the maps.

Six NPCs, one per town job. Each has a short bark set that changes as players clear map milestones, so the town reacts to progress.

## How the story is told in-game

No cutscenes and nothing that stops play. Story is optional: a player who ignores all of it still has a full game.

| Channel | What it is | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notes** | Memos, letters, luggage tags, patient files. Pick up with one button, read later. | Max 80 words. Read aloud by a voice on pickup, with subtitles. Never opens a screen mid-round. |
| **Radio** | Short lines from Pell, the Cordon or the Wick, played in the lull between rounds. | Max 15 seconds. One per lull at most. Always subtitled. |
| **Environment** | Staged rooms, signs, graffiti, Wick symbols, what the Lit wear (lab coats in Halcyon, overalls at the dam, coats at the Junction, gowns at Grauwald). | Every room tells one small story you can read in a glance. |
| **Town barks** | NPC one-liners that change after milestones (first power on, first secret, map unlocked). | 3 to 5 lines per NPC per milestone. |
| **Codex** | Every note and radio line you found, saved per character, on a board in Pell's radio hut. | Sorted by map. Shows how many of each map's entries you have. |
| **Wall of Finds** | Secret cosmetics shown in Bram's museum with a story card. | One card per secret. |

**For the build.** Each lore entry is a data file (`content/lore/<map_id>/<entry_id>.tres`) with id, map, type, trigger, text and audio. Adding lore means adding a file, nothing else, which fits the scale-easily rule. Aim for about 12 notes and 8 radio lines per map, and write all text in short sentences for readability.

## Tone and rules

- **Grounded, cold, a little sad.** Ordinary people in a bad winter, not superheroes. Dark humour from NPCs is fine; no jokey zombies.
- **Show, don't explain.** Never say outright who cut the power. Give clues and let players argue.
- **Colour means something.** Green is Fenlight, orange sodium light is safety, teal is Oriel. Lore and art must agree.
- **Fully original.** No real companies, people or brands. Real region (Ardennes) is fine; every town, firm and person is invented. No real names of the team in any content.
- **Short text.** Sentences under 20 words, notes under 80 words, everything voiced or subtitled.
